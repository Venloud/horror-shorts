FICTION
TITLE: The Trap House

It was a Sunday night on 63rd.

Eagles game on, a few girls over, everybody smoking, eating, arguing over the game.

Regular night.

At halftime, one of the girls asked,

“Where the bathroom at?”

My cousin pointed upstairs.

His boy immediately said,

“Don't go up there.”

Everybody laughed.

She went anyway.

We kept watching the game.

Thirty seconds.

Then a minute.

Then three slow footsteps upstairs.

Step.

Step.

Step.

My cousin yelled,

“You good?”

Nothing.

Then she screamed.

She came flying down the stairs.

“There’s somebody upstairs!”

His boy went completely quiet.

“What did they look like?”

She was shaking.

“I thought it was one of y'all.”

Then she whispered,

“It didn't have a face.”

Nobody was laughing anymore.

Then from upstairs...

we heard her voice.

“Guys?”

She was standing right beside us.

Something started walking down the stairs.

We BOOKED.

Everybody ran out onto 63rd.

We got halfway down the block when somebody yelled,

“WAIT!”

We stopped.

Under the streetlight was the girl.

Barefoot.

Staring at us.

My cousin yelled,

“Yo! Come here!”

She smiled.

Then his boy grabbed my cousin's shoulder.

“Don't.”

My cousin looked at him.

“That's not her.”

Everybody froze.

“Why?”

He pointed back toward the house.

The real girl was standing in the upstairs window.

Pounding on the glass.

Screaming.

Trying to get our attention.

Then the girl under the streetlight smiled wider.

And said,

“Why'd y'all leave me upstairs?”

Then every light in the house turned on at once.

And every window...

had somebody standing in it.
