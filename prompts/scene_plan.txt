# Night Files scene plan instructions.
# Step 2: turns the finished story script into scenes, image prompts, sounds and the caption.
# {script} and {sfx_list} are filled in by the bot. Edit anything else freely.

You turn a finished horror narration script into a scene plan for a vertical TikTok video.

SCRIPT:
"""
{script}
"""

SPLIT IT INTO SCENES
- Split the script, in order, into 7 to 12 scenes of 1-3 sentences each, and put each part in "narration".
- COPY THE WORDS EXACTLY. Do not rewrite, shorten, add or reorder anything. Only remove markdown symbols, headings and quotation marks around dialogue if they would sound odd read aloud.
- Scene 1 must be the script's opening hook.

IMAGE PROMPTS
- Describe each painted frame: subject + setting + lighting + composition (camera angle / shot size).
- FOUR images per scene, one for each quarter of the narration, and each shows EXACTLY what is being said at that moment (when the words say "the bridge", show the bridge; "the windshield", show the view through the windshield): "image_prompt" = first part, "image_prompt_2" = second, "image_prompt_3" = third, "image_prompt_4" = last part. Each is a different shot, like a fast film edit: mix wide establishing shots of the place, medium shots of people, close-ups of key objects and clues, and reaction shots. A short scene may use fewer (leave the extra ones empty).
- Illustrated storybook / animated-film look, NOT a photo. The characters are fictional, so show them clearly with EXPRESSIVE FACES (worry, fear, confusion, relief). Faces sell the emotion.
- CONSISTENCY IS EVERYTHING: viewers must recognise the same people and the same rooms from shot to shot.
  - "characters": list every character once with a short "name" (e.g. "the night guard", "the old neighbor") and a fixed "look" (age, build, hair, exact clothes and colors, one prop; appearance only, never emotions or expressions), e.g. "a young man, mid 20s, short black hair, grey work jacket, flashlight".
  - "locations": list the 1-3 places the story happens with a short "name" (e.g. "the living room") and a fixed "look" (layout, furniture, colors, key objects), e.g. "cozy suburban living room, beige couch, old CRT TV on a wooden stand, tall lamp, staircase in the back".
  - In every image prompt, refer to characters by their exact name ("the night guard shines his flashlight at the old neighbor"). Their look is added automatically, so don't repeat it. Set each scene's "location" to one location name.
  - Keep the same outfits and the same rooms for the whole story unless the story truly moves.
- VARIETY, like a film: only about HALF the shots show a character. The rest are establishing shots of the place, key objects and clues, traces of the threat (a shape in the fog, an open door, footprints), and atmosphere. Characters stay consistent when they appear; they don't need to appear in every frame.
- KEEP EVERY SHOT SIMPLE AND DRAWABLE: one clear subject doing one clear thing, seen from a normal angle. The image AI draws these badly, so never ask for: isolated body parts (boots, feet, hands) without the whole person, extreme low or high angles, a character's face seen from behind, mirror reflections that differ from reality, "two shadows", or anything that only makes sense with a caption. Show the moment before or the reaction instead (e.g. the person staring at the mirror in shock, not the impossible reflection).
- Lighting tells the story: warm, cozy lamp light at the start; darker, colder and more shadowy as the tension rises; darkest at the climax.
- Keep the threat mostly hidden until the climax: a shadow, a shape under the bed, a hand, eyes in the dark. Show it clearly at most once.
- No text or writing in the image. No blood or gore. Nobody is ever shown hurt.
- Scene 1's image must be the most striking, eerie image of the whole story (it is the thumbnail).

SOUND EFFECTS
- A sound only goes on a scene when the narration of THAT scene literally mentions the sound or its source (footsteps, breathing, a whisper, a knock, a door, a phone, a scream, a heartbeat). Otherwise use "none".
- Match the surface and place exactly (footsteps on pine needles = footsteps_leaves; in a hallway = footsteps_hallway).
- 2 to 4 sounds per video at most. Never on scene 1 (the hook). Silence is scarier than a random sound.
- Allowed sounds: {sfx_list}

OTHER FIELDS
- title: 3-7 word internal title.
- premise: one sentence summary of the plot.
- setting: the main place, in a few words (e.g. "hospital basement night shift").
- threat: what the danger really is and what it wants, in one short sentence.
- twist: the key reveal / ending concept, in one short sentence.
- hook_overlay: 2-5 word on-screen text shown big over the first seconds. It must be shocking on its own, not a title, e.g. "It texted from my phone", "I don't have a landlord", "He waved from under the ice". No emojis.
- twist_scene: the 0-based index of the scene where the twist hits.
- caption: line 1 = a searchable story title that says what happens, the way people would type it into TikTok search, e.g. "The Night Guard Checked the Cameras... and Saw Himself". Line 2 = a question that makes people comment (e.g. "Would you have checked?"). Max 180 characters. May use 1 emoji.
- hashtags: 5 hashtags without the # symbol, mixing broad (scarystories, horrortok) and specific.
- pinned_comment: a short comment the creator can pin to start discussion.
