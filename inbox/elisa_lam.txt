Hotel guests said their tap water tasted strange. Nobody looked inside the tanks on the roof for nineteen days. This is a true story.
In 2013, Elisa Lam, a twenty-one-year-old Canadian student, checked into the Cecil Hotel in Los Angeles.
On January thirty-first, she vanished.
Police released the elevator footage. Elisa steps in and presses every button. The doors won't close. She leans out into the hallway, hides in the corner, and waves her hands at someone no one can see.
Millions watched it. Nobody could explain it.
Then guests complained about the water pressure. A worker climbed to the roof and opened one of the tanks.
Elisa was inside.
The coroner ruled it an accidental drowning, linked to her bipolar disorder. Netflix later made a whole series about it.
But for nineteen days, the guests at the Cecil had been drinking and showering in that water.
