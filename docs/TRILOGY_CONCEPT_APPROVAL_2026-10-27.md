## Candidate A, working series bible: The Second Siren

- **Series ID (PROPOSED):** `second-siren-01`. **Category:** original fictional survival horror, not true crime, not historical.
- **Format:** exactly three short episodes, roughly 60–75 seconds of narration where comprehension permits; three small story payoffs plus a final resolution.
- **Central question:** Why does the emergency siren sound a second time from a supposedly sealed pump station?
- **Setting:** an isolated coastal flood-control station at night during a storm, with a windowed control booth looking down a corridor to a flood gate. The town lies above the floodwall. Geography remains consistent.
- **Characters:** **Mara**, adult night operator, short dark hair, yellow rain jacket with one reflective silver band, carries a metal flashlight. **Eli**, adult pump technician, short greying hair, navy coat with patched elbow, carries a spanner. **Control dispatcher** heard through radio only, no invented disembodied face; voice is either an in-world recorded system or original actor voice, not another channel narrator.
- **World rules:** two siren tones exist: the first is a coastal warning sent **outward**. The second is a manual **internal chamber evacuation tone** that can only sound when the lower maintenance passage fills. An old wiring label inaccurately calls it an external test. Manual flood gate A redirects incoming water away from town; bypass lever B temporarily restores power to the chamber siren circuit; pulling B while A is closed allows a brief rescue window but traps the operator outside the lower passage. Establish both controls visually before the final payoff, no supernatural technology invented at the last minute.
- **Important props:** chipped brass floodwheel A; sealed narrow **red test lever B**; scratched glass gauge; a salt-stained maintenance map; the red indicator lamp. Each remains in the same location, no fake numeric tide values.
- **Visual motif (R9):** the **same single red signal lamp** appears only when it conveys a factual-in-fiction change (first siren / lower passage response / restored circuit). It is not a scoreboard, progress percentage or repeating animation loop.
- **Time and space:** one storm night, sequential corridor → control booth → lower passage → exterior. No contradictory time jumps or unrealistic instant travel.
- **Audio:** restrained industrial ambience, one clearly motivated siren, rain, radio click. Sound must come from what the script describes. No compulsory music drop.
- **Look:** cinematic Night Files illustrated realism, deep navy concrete, water reflection, weathered bronze and one red signal point; one strong subject per shot. Maintain existing host/image approval policy, one-second approved cover, hard cuts, safe captions.
- **Continuity ledgers:** Mara keeps the flashlight throughout; Eli's spanner stays with Eli until handed to Mara on-screen if used; valve A does not turn by itself; the test lever remains sealed until Episode 2; the maintenance map is first observed in Episode 1, correctly interpreted in Episode 2, and acted on in Episode 3. Nobody acquires unexplained new knowledge.

### Episode 1: The wrong warning

**Hook options:**
1. “The flood siren sounded twice. The station only had one outdoor alarm.”
2. “Mara was shutting down the floodgate when the second siren started underground.”

Mara hears the routine exterior siren, then a second muffled tone **from below** after the outdoor circuit has been switched off. She checks the gauge and finds the lower maintenance passage filling. **Mini-loop payoff:** the second sound is real, not a recording or outside wind; it originates behind the sealed service door. **New complication:** the maintenance map marks that tone “TEST ONLY” even though the chamber is flooding. **Ending decision:** should Mara open the passage or find what powers the siren? The choice is consequential, not a fake comment vote.

**Narration-to-visual:** outdoor warning light → hand on main switch → darkened booth → red lamp stays illuminated → gauge and lower door → exact “TEST ONLY” label as a **verified in-fiction designed prop**, not inaccurate AI-generated text. Never add unsupported water-depth numerals.

**Cover/title direction:** `The Siren Sounded Twice | Part 1`; a tiny red light glowing beyond a locked flooded door; no redundant headline in frame.

### Episode 2: What the label hid

**Hook options:**
1. “That ‘test’ siren was the only warning the people below could hear.”
2. “The maintenance map left out a switch behind the floodwheel.”

Eli arrives, recalls that the map was printed before lever B was wired. The second siren indicates **people could still be trapped** in the lower corridor, though it does not prove anyone is present. Mara sends a radio call; a knocked pipe answers. **Mini-loop payoff:** the alarm is an internal evacuation signal, the label is outdated. **New consequence:** opening the service door immediately would send water into the control booth. Their choices narrow to the two controls introduced earlier. **Ending:** Eli offers to stay at wheel A while Mara operates B, but the radio loses contact.

**Narration-to-visual:** map and lever with accurate labels as designed overlay → adult technician demonstrates blocked passage → authentic radio contact attempt → closed wheel / passage flooding. Use an optional tiny red lamp motif, not a UI danger meter.

**Cover/title direction:** `The Forgotten Switch | Part 2`; brass wheel beside a small red lever, rainwater behind a window.

### Episode 3: The person inside

**Hook options:**
1. “They finally knew what the second siren meant. Now someone had to answer it.”
2. “Mara could save the lower passage, but only by turning off its alarm.”

Mara opens bypass B while Eli holds wheel A. The warning returns long enough to guide an adult maintenance worker from the chamber to the safe upper stairs. **Climax:** we learn the "answering knocks" were a trapped worker using a pipe; the second siren had been deliberately activated from below. The station's operator rescues the worker, and the flood gate redirects the surge, but manual lever B is water-damaged beyond reset. **Final consequence:** when daylight arrives, the lamp goes out, not because the danger was imaginary but because the internal evacuation channel has finally been cleared. They report the false “TEST ONLY” label for replacement. The opening promise is answered, and there is no fourth installment.

**Narration-to-visual:** established wheel A / lever B working together → only the known corridor geometry → faint pipe reply → adult worker ascending → gauge settling / daylight / report slip with **script-supported** “REPLACE LABEL” text. A quieter resolution may serve better than a beat drop.

**Cover/title direction:** `The Answer Below | Part 3`; Mara at the original floodgate as the corridor beyond is lit for the first time.

## Candidate B, short alternative: The Empty Seat Route

At the night-bus terminal, operator **Jonah** finds one seat warm and dry on a bus whose windows and other seats are soaked. Driver **Rae** reports no passengers. A single authentic punch-hole ticket drops from the overhead route box when the bus crosses a certain streetlight. **Part 1 payoff:** the empty seat tracks a real stop despite the bus being declared out of service. **Part 2 reversal:** the ticket belongs to an emergency evacuation route erased after a road collapse; someone has been sending **unofficial distress signals**, not conducting a haunting. **Part 3 resolution:** following the paper ticket's marked turn lets Rae find stranded maintenance workers and leads to a verified safe route. The last empty seat stays empty as a reminder, not a fabricated supernatural reveal. Fixed set: same bus, terminal clock, dry seat, route placard, two adult staff. No borrowed competitor names, rules, shots, costumes or music.
