## Two original choices

### Concept A, recommended: The Return Shelf

**One-line promise:** A night clerk in a small municipal lost-property office discovers that every orange return tag predicts an object that will disappear, until the next tag names her colleague.

**Why this wins:** One visually repeatable set with distinctive practical props, readable numbered tags and scanner receipts, two adults with fixed looks, a clear physical rule that evolves logically, and a three-episode central problem with independent demonstrations. Original illustrated noir/magenta Night Files identity; no cruise, soldiers, high-rise blackout or copied survival world. Low asset budget compared with the alternate.

**Tone:** intimate bureaucratic supernatural horror, quiet radio/static, restraint and observational choices rather than gore. Explicitly **original fiction**. Time, stamp and scanner text are fictional story props, not evidence of a real disappearance.

### Concept B, alternative: The Orchard Bell

**One-line promise:** A rural irrigation technician finds bells attached to buried roots ringing in exactly the order that the orchard's trees are going to fall, except a bell starts ringing for her house.

**Series escalation:** Ep1 verifies the bells predict tree collapses rather than cause them; Ep2 the collapsing roots reveal a decades-old drainage trench from the farm toward the home, changing the actual threat; Ep3 she opens a drainage bypass using previously established tools but loses the orchard, and the last bell belongs to a tree that never existed. A quieter ecological/mechanical dread piece with greater environment/asset variety. **Original fiction.**

**Recommendation:** Concept A. Better continuity, more immediately understandable stakes, fewer new illustrated locations and prop reuse. This is a creative recommendation, not evidence of improved platform retention.

## Three-episode outline, self-contained intermediate payoffs

### Episode 1, “Tomorrow's Lost Property”

**Hook options:**
- “The umbrella hadn't been lost yet. Its return tag had a time.”
- “At 2:13, an umbrella vanished from a locked shelf.”
- “A clerk found tomorrow's lost property in tonight's office.”

**Central question:** Do the orange return tags predict an object's loss? **Rising action / B-roll:** the silent return slot, label printer, analog 2:12 clock, Leena marks the umbrella/tag, empty locked shelf. **First payoff:** at 2:13, umbrella disappears; tags accurately predict when *objects* disappear. **Second conflict:** Tom's worn satchel arrives tagged 2:21 while he is carrying his satchel, so now the loss is imminent and personal. **End decision:** Leena must decide whether scanning RETURNED would save it.

**Narration-to-visual plan (not final script):** immediate close-up of orange `2:13` tag → real umbrella silhouette on shelf → close analog clock and Leena's still hand → hard cut to empty shelf at matching time → Tom's worn satchel and identical strap buckle → orange `2:21` tag, no generic dark-hallway filler. Keep a small legible `VOID BEFORE DUE` ledger in the office visual as an *established* later payoff. The viewer learns a rule in this episode and does not need Part 2 to understand the experiment.

**Cover:** the approved one-second Night Files cover treatment, close orange time-tag with small umbrella silhouette, title text “LOST TOMORROW,” not a competitor-style recreation.

### Episode 2, “Return to Owner”

**Hook options:**
- “Returning the satchel erased the wrong thing.”
- “The scanner said RETURNED. Tom was suddenly gone.”
- “The bag was safe. The person who owned it wasn't.”

**Central question:** What does scanning RETURNED actually do? **Rising action / B-roll:** Tom confirms the strap mark, Leena tries the scanner, screen reads RETURNED, Tom's raincoat remains on a chair but Tom is absent, result slip prints TRANSFERRED LOSS: OWNER. **First payoff:** premature returns move the impending loss from property to owner. **Second conflict:** the original 2:21 deadline is close and the result receipt can't be deleted by the ordinary scanner button. **End decision:** Leena remembers the previously visible carbon ledger and reaches for the matching page.

**Narration-to-visual plan:** camera follows satchel from Tom's hand to scanner → tactile ease-out warning button reveal (not between shots) → hard cut to chair and empty hallway → close exact scanner text rather than a fabricated authority document → ticking deadline if the audio beat warrants it → carbon duplicate behind scanner. Maintain Leena's bun/cardigan and Tom's satchel; show the coat with no gore.

**Cover:** same approved one-second Night Files treatment, empty chair, scanner result “RETURNED,” title “THE WRONG RETURN.”

### Episode 3, “Void Before Due”

**Hook options:**
- “She had forty seconds to undo one wrong return.”
- “The receipt could bring Tom back, but not the satchel.”
- “The loss was already assigned to Tom. The clock hadn't reached 2:21.”

**Central question:** Can Leena reverse the consequence before the deadline? **Rising action / B-roll:** match the receipt's orange transaction number to a carbon page, analog seconds passing, scanner's blocked action, Leena operates the established VOID BEFORE DUE mechanism. **Climax/payoff:** on a legitimate void before 2:21, Tom reappears and the satchel vanishes on time. The solution uses **the rule planted in Episode 1** rather than an invented new power. **Resolution:** Leena understands why the return shelf isn't saving anything, and they leave without processing the next arrival. A clear ending, not a demand for a fourth episode.

**Narration-to-visual plan:** close-up of carbon copy and scanner original tag → precise matched serial digits → Leena presses the existing VOID button, one restrained low heartbeat of silence rather than mandatory swell → hard cut to Tom back in the chair → satchel gone at 2:21 → Leena shuts the ledger, static shelf, final short line. No fake shock flash or narrative-new-rule at the finish.

**Cover:** same one-second approved cover, a hand on the red VOID stamp under `2:21`, title “VOID BEFORE DUE.”
