## Series bible
Series: no-safe-harbor-01, original fictional zombie apocalypse. Night Files remains standalone by default.
Perspective: second-person, viewer aboard a 10-meter motor cruiser several miles offshore. Crew of five including viewer, speaking survivors: captain Nora (40s, weathered face, short gray-flecked hair, green rain jacket), deckhand Malik (late 20s, dark skin, shaved head, orange life vest), and medic June (30s, straight black bob, navy fleece); fourth crewmate Sam (30s, curly brown hair, yellow jacket). Visual depiction consistent, no new characters without introduction.
Boat continuity: white cabin cruiser with teal stripe, wheelhouse front, narrow aft deck, working VHF set, analog fuel gauge, tank water-level gauge, limited bottled drinking water, basic fishing gear, life jackets, one service dinghy. No convenient infinite fuel, power, bait, medical supplies or rain.
Infection rule: infected people remain dangerous, but the virus does NOT spread across seawater via magical waves. Bites/close contact are treated as threats, speculative transmission never announced as confirmed science. Mainland media is unreliable as services collapse. Survivors do not conclusively know if all land is unsafe.
Timeline: Ep1 hour 0; Ep2 approximately 48 hours later; Ep3 following island lead. Fuel stays low after Ep1, therefore crew uses engine sparingly, drifts with sea anchor when conditions permit, and travels only a nearby, geographically plausible island hop after acquiring fuel on trawler. Storm threat requires preparation, cannot be ignored.
Underlying question: how can five people survive offshore without safely returning to land for essential supplies?
Narrative motifs: VHF radio static, coastline dimming behind boat, a marked water bottle and low fuel gauge. No fabricated documentary evidence, fake verified news or false historical claims.
Visual style: existing Night Files illustrated cinematic identity, vertical 9:16, approved one-second cover, hard cuts by default; practical one- or two-second radio/survival inserts only where narrated, restrained ambient noise and caption safe zones.

## Episode 1, DON'T RETURN TO SHORE
Proposed full narration, approx 150 words:
"The first radio alert told you to return to shore. Ten minutes later, it told you never to come back.
You're fishing offshore with four friends when the music stops. A newsreader reports a new virus and violent attacks at the harbor. Then a second station interrupts with evacuation orders. Nobody can say where to evacuate.
The marine radio lights up. Mayday after mayday. One captain says his crew has locked themselves inside the bridge. Another call ends with a scream and an open microphone.
Nora turns the boat toward land, until the Coast Guard broadcasts one last clear instruction: all civilian vessels must remain offshore. The harbor entrance is closed.
Through binoculars, you see smoke beyond the breakwater. No one aboard knows how far the outbreak has spread.
Malik checks the fuel gauge. Less than a quarter remains. June counts the drinking water.
And out west, a storm is building.
You can stay away from land tonight. But you can't stay out here forever."

Shot plan: (1) radio dial and hand reaching for knob, (2) chart showing vessel offshore without false coordinates, (3) marine radio channel indicator and successive source-free fictional distress voices, (4) Nora at helm changing heading, (5) distant harbor smoke seen through binoculars, (6) fuel gauge below quarter and water supply, (7) storm front on horizon. Local payoff: verified within fiction that the harbor is closed; remaining threat is sustainability. Hook visual immediately matches radio alert.
Cover: DON'T RETURN, boat silhouette and VHF distress light; small fiction designation in metadata.

## Episode 2, THE OCEAN ISN'T SAFE EITHER
Proposed full narration, approx 145 words:
"By the second morning, you know something worse than the screams on the radio: silence.
Nora kept the engine off through the night to save fuel. The storm pushed you off course, and the last weather forecast is already out of date.
Five people, a handful of drinking bottles, and no safe port. Malik says you might catch fish, but fish won't refill the freshwater tank.
Then a trawler appears, drifting across your path. No navigation lights. No answer to three radio calls.
From the deck you can see fuel cans, and a hose coiled beside the wheelhouse. You approach in the dinghy while June watches through binoculars.
There are no moving figures aboard. Malik checks the cabin without opening any sealed compartment. You take the accessible water containers and enough fuel to make a short passage.
A recording starts on its radio: 'Evacuation point, Gull Island. Do not approach mainland docks.'
Gull Island is on Nora's chart.
But the recording never says when it was made."

Shot plan: (1) static-filled VHF and dawn sea, (2) engine shutdown and drifting yacht with storm residue, (3) marked drinking bottles and water gauge, (4) drifting dark trawler at a safe distance, (5) careful dinghy boarding and watchful crew, (6) accessible supply containers and no unexplained confrontation, (7) chart with Gull Island marked and old radio recording. Local payoff: acquired modest supplies and a possible destination; uncertainty remains. Avoid implying dead crew or virus status without evidence.

## Episode 3, THE LAST SAFE PLACE
Proposed full narration, approx 145 words:
"The island was supposed to be your way back to land. Instead, its harbor is full of abandoned boats.
You approach slowly, with the engine barely above idle. No one answers the marine radio. A ferry floats against its mooring with every door open.
Nora keeps the cruiser outside the harbor. From the dinghy you can see movement near the terminal: infected people, clustered around the only fuel dock.
But beyond the dock, rain barrels sit beside an empty maintenance shed. Malik spots sealed drinking-water bottles through its window.
You wait until the infected drift toward a distant alarm, then make one short landing. June stays in the dinghy. You and Malik take only what you can carry, and leave before the figures turn back.
Back aboard, Nora records the location and the risk. Rain can supplement your water, fishing might help, and fuel will be used only when necessary.
The island was not safe.
Neither was the ocean.
But tonight, you have another chance."

Shot plan: (1) island harbor with abandoned boats, (2) exterior ferry and unanswered radio, (3) visible infected at dock, shed and barrels established in one geographic shot, (4) survivors watching from dinghy, (5) short carefully grounded landing retrieving sealed water, (6) safe withdrawal with no miraculous extraction, (7) nightly ration log and distant dark island. Local payoff: short-term replenishment, not an unrealistic permanent maritime solution. Fictional infected and alarm movement must be established visually and causally, not random teleportation.
Cover: NO SAFE LAND, boat outside abandoned port.
