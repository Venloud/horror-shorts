TRUE SCRIPT
The world's most famous masterpiece vanished from a locked museum without leaving a single clue.
On August 21, 1911, an amateur painter arrived at the Louvre to find Leonardo da Vinci’s Mona Lisa gone.
The woman with the haunting half-smile was missing, baffling French detectives for more than two years.
Desperate investigators mistakenly hauled in poet Guillaume Apollinaire and artist Pablo Picasso to crack the case.
At one point, even American tycoon J. P. Morgan fell under suspicion for commissioning the theft.
In December 1913, an Italian house painter contacted a prominent Florence art dealer, claiming he had it.
Police swooped in, arresting former museum employee Vincenzo Peruggia and safely recovering the celebrated masterpiece.
Peruggia served six months in jail, having removed the painting and spirited it out beneath his clothes.
