TRUE SCRIPT
He ordered a bourbon, handed the flight attendant a note, and jumped out of a jet with two hundred thousand dollars. This is a true story.
November 1971. A quiet man in a dark suit boards a flight from Portland to Seattle. His ticket says Dan Cooper.
The note says he has a bomb. He opens his briefcase. Red sticks. Wires.
His demands: two hundred thousand dollars and four parachutes.
In Seattle, he trades the passengers for the money, then sends the plane back into the sky, toward Mexico.
Somewhere over the forests of Washington, the rear stairs open.
Cooper jumps into the dark and the freezing wind.
He is never seen again.
In 1980, an eight-year-old boy digging by the Columbia River finds bundles of rotting twenty-dollar bills.
Cooper's money.
The rest of it is still out there. And maybe, so is he.
