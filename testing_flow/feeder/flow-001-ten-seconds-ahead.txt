The first knock came from inside the closet. Everybody heard it, but nobody moved.

It was Sunday night on Sixty-Third Street. Football on the TV, wings on the table, my cousins arguing over a missed field goal. Two girls from down the block were over, and the whole house smelled like somebody had burned the popcorn.

Then three knocks came from the hallway closet.

My cousin Dre laughed. Said it was the pipes.

But the closet door had been taken off its hinges last summer.

We all turned. The doorway was empty.

Then my phone lit up. A video from an unknown number. It showed us sitting in the living room, right now, filmed from the hallway.

I zoomed in. Behind our couch stood a tall figure with its head bent sideways.

Nobody was behind us when I looked.

The TV went black. In the reflection, the figure was closer.

Then Dre whispered, "Don't turn around."

I asked how he knew.

He pointed at his phone. Same video. But his was ten seconds ahead.

On his screen, we had already turned around.

And every single one of us was gone.
